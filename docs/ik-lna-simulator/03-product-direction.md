# Product direction: beyond operational training needs

The pilot is deliberately bounded: one function, one gap, causes you can find in a day. Future
scenarios must simulate **organisational** challenges, where the request for training is usually
a symptom of strategy execution, operating-model or leadership problems.

## Scenario design standard (applies to every new scenario)

1. **A strategic layer.** At least one executive who owns the strategy. Explicit priorities that
   conflict. The business stake is quantified in the scenario data.
2. **Multi-level causation.** True causes span at least three of: strategy, operating model and
   structure, process, technology, governance and incentives, leadership, culture, capability. At
   Intermediate and Advanced level, individual skill gaps carry **no more than 30%** of the cause
   weight, and at least one scenario per tier has **no training in the right answer at all**.
3. **The request is a distractor.** The stated solution, and the requester's explanation of the
   cause, are plausible and partly supported, so users must weigh evidence rather than dismiss them.
4. **Conflicting perspectives with explicit biases.** Hidden insights are gated by rapport and
   by evidence from other stakeholders, never by asking more questions.
5. **Problem framing first.** Every scenario defines framing options, including a performance-gap
   framing, a solution-first framing, and cause assumptions.
6. **Impact, not activity.** The KPI model separates learning effectiveness from organisational
   impact, with baselines, targets and owners defined in the data (Phase 3).

These rules are enforced by the scenario validator where they can be checked mechanically.

## Difficulty ladder

| Level | Scope | Stakeholders | Evidence | Typical true causes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | One function | 5–6 | Mostly consistent | Process, technology, incentives, a bounded skill gap |
| Intermediate | Cross-functional programme | 7–8 | Conflicting | Operating model, governance, change management, leadership alignment |
| Advanced | Enterprise strategy or transformation | 8–10, with politics | Incomplete, contradictory, some withheld | Strategy-execution system, decision rights, capital allocation, culture, leadership pipeline |

## Proposed Phase 2 flagships (for your approval)

- **Intermediate: "The Platform Nobody Uses"** (Strategy & Transformation). ERP adoption is at
  34% eight months after go-live. The CEO says execution discipline, the CHRO says leadership
  capability, the COO says the operating model, and frontline staff point to duplicate paper and
  system workflows. True causes: the operating model was never redesigned for the system; KPIs
  still reward legacy throughput; the super-user network was defunded. A broad leadership
  programme is a harmful distractor.
- **Advanced: "The Strategy That Stalled"** (Strategy execution / operating model). A
  diversified group launched a growth strategy two years ago. The CEO asks for a "strategic
  leadership programme" for the top 150 leaders. Underneath: 40 initiatives with no portfolio
  prioritisation; unclear decision rights between the holding company and business units;
  strategic KPIs not cascaded into scorecards; capital allocation unchanged; and a genuine
  commercial and digital capability gap in one business unit. There is visible tension between the
  Chief Strategy Officer and two business-unit heads, and the CFO has evidence nobody has asked
  for. Leadership alignment, governance and operating-model interventions dominate; training is a
  minor, targeted element.

**Later phases:** AI adoption and workforce readiness, succession readiness, culture
transformation after a merger, regulatory transformation, and strategic workforce planning for a
new business line. Each will be built to the same standard.
